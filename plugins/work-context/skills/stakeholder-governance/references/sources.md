# Source coverage

Load for bootstrap, full-profile research, document review or refresh. For targeted work, use only the sources needed to establish the requested contribution and its context.

## Contents

1. Bound the investigation
2. Source surfaces
3. Discover, resolve, reconcile
4. Consume the archive's controls
5. Coverage statuses

## 1. Bound the investigation

- Work from the project's local files and supplied material. Begin with supplied material and the existing workspace archive: index and contexts for navigation, then the primary passages in context. Compare the archive's actual coverage with the requested range and report meeting gaps for meetings-governance.
- Then screen the rest of the project inventory (`30 Sources`, `20 Working`, `10 Issued Outputs` and relevant retained history) for documents, reports, presentations and correspondence bearing on the entity and the project's purpose, and read the relevant content. Treat archived and earlier versions as history, not current authority.
- An archive is not a prerequisite. For a genuinely blank workspace, read the user's supplied sources in standalone mode, citing passages, without recreating the capture system. Do not widen an archive's scope or initialise a full archive merely to complete a profile.
- First-time backfill uses the requested range; if none, set and state a sensible project-relevant range, asking only when materially ambiguous.
- Refresh reads new or changed archive outputs and project files beyond the last actually reviewed cutoff, and the passages a changed document affects rather than the whole collection. Do not silently substitute a short recent window for historical research, or assume old messages were never edited.
- External research is outside the default scope. Email, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive, directory and public searches are available only when the user separately authorises them for a stated question; then search only as relevant to the named entities and project, and do not import unrelated personal information, communication histories or sensitive attributes.
- Existing opt-in folder boundaries still apply; a source category is not permission to scan excluded folders, private repositories or unrelated mailboxes.

## 2. Source surfaces

A full initial profile considers each project-file row through its owning workflow; an existing archive can satisfy several rows without new collection. "All appropriate sources" means relevant surfaces within scope, not every system.

### Project files (default)

| Surface | What to look for | Attribution and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User-supplied evidence and project files | Named files first, then relevant documents, reports, presentations, notes and correspondence copies across Sources, Working, Issued Outputs and retained history; dated signatures and stated roles; match supplied meeting material to existing records and report new capture needs to meetings-governance. | Read the content, not only the title or metadata. Separate originals from summaries and earlier versions from current ones. User recollections are attributed reports, not independent confirmation. |
| Meetings archive | Consume the archive's resolved occurrences, companions, attendance and resource pointers; report gaps for meetings-governance. | No parallel calendar/call/transcript discovery. Agendas and invitations show planned activity, not speech or attendance. A retained resource records retention, not that its content was read. |
| Saved correspondence and chats | Meeting-linked email and Teams chats already saved locally, and independently supplied correspondence. | Separate new text from quoted history; forwarded claims keep their reporting chain. Never re-page an archived thread. Reactions, membership and brief assent are not blanket agreement. |
| Notes and collaborative artefacts | Saved notes, minutes, Loop, whiteboard, Q&A and recaps. | Notes may be reporting or synthesis, not verbatim speech; keep authorship and links to primary evidence. |
| Links and pointers outside the project | Remote documents, pages and shared items cited by project files. | Leads only. Record them as not checked unless their review is separately authorised; do not follow them. |

### Authorised supplementary research (only when separately authorised)

| Surface | What to look for | Attribution and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email and Teams | Relevant independently authored non-meeting correspondence, chats and channel conversations not already saved in the project. | Separate new text from quoted history. A recent-chat list is not full history; never re-page an archived thread. |
| SharePoint and OneDrive | Remote copies and versions of documents the project cites, and independent authored sections, comments or changes not yet reviewed. | A latest editor is not necessarily the author. Record missing comment/version access; hand back meeting-resource gaps. |
| Current-work and business systems | Planner/To Do, SharePoint Lists, decision logs or CRM records explicitly tied to the entity and project, via structured reads and the appropriate domain skill. | Assignment is not acceptance; a record's creator or owner is not necessarily the speaker or decision-maker. The profile is not a parallel task or CRM system. |
| Identity and role | Authorised directory records, official biographies, explicit role/authority statements. | Verify as of the relevant date. Seniority, charts and titles do not prove influence, decision rights or endorsement. |
| Public first-party evidence | Official publications, authorised organisational statements, public speeches, articles and biographies bearing on the project. | Corroborate identity, authority and dates; distinguish institutional positions from individual remarks. Do not mine unrelated personal social content or send private source text, internal project details or sensitive search terms to public services. |

Not every surface is compulsory. Mark immaterial ones not applicable, but do not silently omit a relevant project surface because another source gave a plausible answer.

## 3. Discover, resolve, reconcile

1. Build a small in-memory set of evidenced entity-name variants, organisation/role context, project aliases, dated meeting titles and known threads. Never invent email addresses or identities.
2. Search project files and archive contents by project topic as well as entity. Use remote discovery only when separately authorised.
3. Resolve important claims to the exact existing source and read the surrounding passage. For a long document, read the sections bearing on the entity and the project's purpose, and name the sections read when it was only partly read. For authorised supplementary sources, semantic results are leads; confirm with exact reads or file-URL grounding.
4. Open the retained originals that existing resource pointers lead to and read the relevant content. Do not recapture the corpus, and hand missing meeting-linked items back. A pointer to a remote-only item is a lead; record it as not checked.
5. Look specifically for corrections, dissent, retractions, later responses and counter-evidence. Do not overweight the most vocal person or mistake copied wording for an independent pattern.
6. Compare chronology and attribution across surfaces before writing. Match source to claim: speech evidence for what was said, role evidence for identity, an authoritative decision record for formal approval.

## 4. Consume the archive's controls

- Check each context's resolved occurrence, source identities, parts, attribution limitations, resource dispositions and `evidence_checked_at`, and carry those qualifications into the profile rather than recreating the procedures.
- Use the context's **topic map** and anchored **key points** to find entity-relevant contributions, then read the anchored passages in the saved transcript. A key-point paraphrase is navigation, not a substitute for the passage when quoting or characterising a person or organisation.
- A context's **Observations** are meeting-level **Inferred** material; never promote them to a person's or organisation's position. **Owner notes** are the archive owner's reported view: useful context, attributed to the owner, never independent evidence of what an entity said, meant or approved.
- Read the relevant archived primary passages, not just the context summary.
- A context's resource table records retained originals and pointers, not their content. Read the relevant retained original before relying on it, or record it as unread and why.
- If a completeness claim matters and the archive does not support it, request the bounded producer handoff; never enumerate transcript objects or chat pages yourself.
- Distinguish promised resources from reviewed evidence, and retain useful existing material when current access is worse.
- Do not copy the meeting skill's classification system, index, manifests or reconciliation cadence into entity analysis.
- For authorised supplementary sources, follow their pagination and disclose incomplete results.
- For an authorised producer handoff, use the client's available Meetings Governance skill, or an explicitly identified local copy, then resume analysis from its outputs. Do not assume the installed package lives inside the target workspace. Legacy meeting paths are readable evidence, not permanent alternative capture destinations; structural migration needs approval.
- Ask before broadening workspace or entity scope, and record the clarified scope in the existing profile or research map.

## 5. Coverage statuses

Record coverage in one compact table in the profile header or research map:

| Source surface | Owner / existing archive link | Status | Range or records checked | Material gap or handoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Reviewed** | The stated bounded records were examined; not a claim about every record in that system. |
| **Partial** | Only part of the relevant source set, pages or content was examined. |
| **Unavailable** | The attempted source could not be accessed; give the observed cause. |
| **Not checked** | Deliberately out of this pass or not yet attempted; give the reason when material. |
| **No relevant evidence found** | A successful, stated bounded search or read found none; not proof of global absence. |
| **Not applicable** | The surface does not bear on the scoped question. |

- These are coverage statuses, not information types or confidence scores, and are distinct from meeting-source coverage: `Reviewed` may mean one excerpt was read while other sources remain unreviewed or unretained; retaining all in-scope sources does not establish attribution, truth or approval.
- Apply them to documents by what was read. A retained or linked document whose content was not read is **Not checked** (retained, not read), never **Reviewed**. A document that could not be opened or extracted is **Unavailable** with the observed cause; one read in part is **Partial**, naming the sections read. A remote-only pointer that was not followed is **Not checked** (outside the authorised scope). Say in the gap column when a source was read but not used and the omission matters.
- A profile or refresh is not described as a complete review while a material relevant source is Not checked, Unavailable or Partial; name each one and its reason.
- Record the research cutoff and latest source date separately when they differ. If profile and map have different coverage, say so.
- Distinguish what the producer retrieved, reviewed and retained. Do not refresh an entity's position or advance a profile's review cutoff merely because the map gained a new producer link.
