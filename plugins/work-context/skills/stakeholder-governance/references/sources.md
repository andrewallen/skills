# Source coverage

Load for bootstrap, full-profile research or refresh. For targeted work, use only the sources needed to establish the requested contribution and its context.

## Contents

1. Bound the investigation
2. Source surfaces
3. Discover, resolve, reconcile
4. Consume the archive's controls
5. Coverage statuses

## 1. Bound the investigation

- Begin with supplied material and the existing workspace archive: index and contexts for navigation, then the primary passages in context. Compare the archive's actual coverage with the requested range and hand meeting gaps to meetings-governance.
- An archive is not a prerequisite. For a genuinely blank workspace, read the user's supplied sources and relevant independent communications in standalone mode, citing passages, without recreating the capture system. Ask only if fuller capture is necessary and not authorised. Do not widen an archive's scope or initialise a full archive merely to complete a profile.
- First-time backfill uses the requested range; if none, set and state a sensible workspace-relevant range, asking only when materially ambiguous.
- Refresh reads new or changed archive outputs and independent sources beyond the last actually reviewed cutoff. Do not silently substitute a short recent window for historical research, or assume old messages were never edited.
- Search email, Teams and documents only as relevant to the named entities and workspace. Do not import unrelated personal information, communication histories or sensitive attributes.
- Existing opt-in folder boundaries still apply; a source category is not permission to scan excluded folders, private repositories or unrelated mailboxes.

## 2. Source surfaces

A full initial profile considers each row through its owning workflow; an existing archive can satisfy several rows without new collection. "All appropriate sources" means relevant surfaces within scope, not every system.

| Surface | What to look for | Attribution and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User-supplied evidence and workspace files | Named files and existing archive/resource pointers first; match supplied meeting material to existing records and hand new capture to meetings-governance. | Separate originals from summaries. User recollections are attributed reports, not independent confirmation. |
| Calendar, meetings, ad hoc calls | Consume the archive's resolved occurrences, companions, attendance and pointers; request a bounded handoff for gaps. | No parallel calendar/call/transcript discovery. Agendas and invitations show planned activity, not speech or attendance. |
| Email | Archived meeting-linked email first; then relevant independently authored non-meeting correspondence (incoming, sent, archived) not already covered. | Separate new text from quoted history; forwarded claims keep their reporting chain. |
| Teams chats and channels | Captured meeting chats first; independent workspace conversations only for uncovered supplementary evidence. | Never re-page an archived thread. A recent-chat list is not full history; reactions, membership and brief assent are not blanket agreement. |
| SharePoint and OneDrive | Existing resource copies/pointers and versions; then independent authored sections, comments, decision records or changes not yet reviewed. | A latest editor is not necessarily the author. Record missing comment/version access; hand back meeting-resource gaps. |
| Notes and collaborative artefacts | Archived notes, minutes, Loop, whiteboard, Q&A and recaps; independently supplied non-meeting notes. | Notes may be reporting or synthesis, not verbatim speech; keep authorship and links to primary evidence. |
| Current-work and business systems | Planner/To Do, SharePoint Lists, decision logs or CRM records explicitly tied to the entity and workspace, via structured reads and the appropriate domain skill. | Assignment is not acceptance; a record's creator or owner is not necessarily the speaker or decision-maker. The profile is not a parallel task or CRM system. |
| Identity and role | Authorised directory records, dated signatures, official biographies, explicit role/authority statements. | Verify as of the relevant date. Seniority, charts and titles do not prove influence, decision rights or endorsement. |
| Public first-party evidence | Official publications, authorised organisational statements, public speeches, articles and biographies bearing on the workspace. | Corroborate identity, authority and dates; distinguish institutional positions from individual remarks. Do not mine unrelated personal social content or send private source text, internal workspace details or sensitive search terms to public services. |

Not every surface is compulsory. Mark immaterial ones not applicable, but do not silently omit a relevant surface because another source gave a plausible answer.

## 3. Discover, resolve, reconcile

1. Build a small in-memory set of evidenced entity-name variants, organisation/role context, workspace aliases, dated meeting titles and known threads. Never invent email addresses or identities.
2. Search archive contents by workspace and topic as well as entity. Use remote discovery only for supplementary evidence or to scope a bounded gap for the meeting owner.
3. Resolve important claims to the exact existing source and read the surrounding passage. For supplementary sources, semantic results are leads; confirm with exact reads or file-URL grounding.
4. Follow existing resource links and relevant independent replies without recapturing the corpus; hand missing meeting-linked items back.
5. Look specifically for corrections, dissent, retractions, later responses and counter-evidence. Do not overweight the most vocal person or mistake copied wording for an independent pattern.
6. Compare chronology and attribution across surfaces before writing. Match source to claim: speech evidence for what was said, role evidence for identity, an authoritative decision record for formal approval.

## 4. Consume the archive's controls

- Check each context's resolved occurrence, source identities, parts, attribution limitations, resource dispositions and `evidence_checked_at`, and carry those qualifications into the profile rather than recreating the procedures.
- Use the context's **topic map** and anchored **key points** to find entity-relevant contributions, then read the anchored passages in the saved transcript. A key-point paraphrase is navigation, not a substitute for the passage when quoting or characterising a person or organisation.
- A context's **Observations** are meeting-level **Inferred** material; never promote them to a person's or organisation's position. **Owner notes** are the archive owner's reported view: useful context, attributed to the owner, never independent evidence of what an entity said, meant or approved.
- Read the relevant archived primary passages, not just the context summary.
- If a completeness claim matters and the archive does not support it, request the bounded producer handoff; never enumerate transcript objects or chat pages yourself.
- Distinguish promised resources from reviewed evidence, and retain useful existing material when current access is worse.
- Do not copy the meeting skill's classification system, index, manifests or reconciliation cadence into entity analysis.
- For directly researched supplementary sources, follow their pagination and disclose incomplete results.
- For an authorised producer handoff, use the client's available Meetings Governance skill, or an explicitly identified local copy, then resume analysis from its outputs. Do not assume the installed package lives inside the target workspace. Legacy meeting paths are readable evidence, not permanent alternative capture destinations; structural migration needs approval.
- Ask before broadening workspace or entity scope, and record the clarified scope in the existing profile or research map.

## 5. Coverage statuses

Record coverage in one compact table in the profile header or research map:

| Source surface | Owner / existing archive link | Status | Range or records checked | Material gap or handoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Reviewed** | The stated bounded records were examined; not a claim about every record in that system. |
| **Partial** | Only part of the relevant source set, pages or content was examined. |
| **Unavailable** | The attempted source could not be accessed; give the observed cause. |
| **Not checked** | Deliberately out of this pass or not yet attempted; give the reason when material. |
| **No relevant evidence found** | A successful, stated bounded search or read found none; not proof of global absence. |
| **Not applicable** | The surface does not bear on the scoped question. |

- These are coverage statuses, not information types or confidence scores, and are distinct from meeting-source coverage: `Reviewed` may mean one excerpt was read while other sources remain unreviewed or unretained; retaining all in-scope sources does not establish attribution, truth or approval.
- Record the research cutoff and latest source date separately when they differ. If profile and map have different coverage, say so.
- Distinguish what the producer retrieved, reviewed and retained. Do not refresh an entity's position or advance a profile's review cutoff merely because the map gained a new producer link.
