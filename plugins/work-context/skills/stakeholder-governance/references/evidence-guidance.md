# Stakeholder evidence guidance

Use evidence to support a bounded profile, not to reconstruct a whole person or organisation.

## Information types

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| Captured | Original communication, recording or retained source; the source may contain errors. |
| Extracted | A selected quotation, discrete fact or close attributed paraphrase with no additional conclusion. |
| Synthesised | Statements organised, condensed or connected within or across sources. |
| Inferred | Interpretation extending beyond an explicit statement. |
| Proposed | A recommendation or application suggested by the author or agent. |

Keep information type separate from attribution, lifecycle, approval, sensitivity and source coverage.

## Attribution

- Preserve the named speaker or author and any reporting intermediary.
- Use exact surrounding passages before quoting or characterising a stakeholder.
- Anonymous or mixed-speaker evidence supports qualified meeting-level synthesis, not personal testimony.
- Several copies of one statement are not independent corroboration.
- A captured proposal is evidence that the person or organisation proposed it. It is not evidence of approval or implementation.
- One employee's statement is not an organisation's formal position without evidenced authority or corroborating institutional evidence.
- Preserve disagreement and differing positions within an organisation.

## Source use

- Work from the project's own files and supplied material. Prefer existing governed meeting evidence, then read the relevant primary content of documents, reports and presentations. A title, metadata, search snippet or retained pointer is not review.
- Lead with evidence relevant to the project's purpose. Include wider relationship context found in the project at lower prominence, and do not present an adjacent contribution as a project requirement.
- Microsoft and Microsoft employees do not receive profiles. Keep their attributed statements as source evidence, and use them to analyse a counterpart where relevant.
- Treat links and pointers to material outside the project as leads. Use directory information, public first-party evidence or other external research only when separately authorised for a stated question; then use directory information for identity and role, not opinions or influence.
- Do not mine unrelated personal material or create broad biographies.
- Record the source range, review cutoff and any access, reading or attribution gap.

## Corrections and updates

- Keep original and corrected accounts distinguishable by date and authority.
- Update only the passages affected by new evidence.
- Advance the evidence cutoff only to the material actually reviewed.
- Preserve valid content when access is partial and state the limitation.
- A thin or partial profile is acceptable when labelled as such. Name each material unread source and why, and do not describe a review as complete while relevant available content remains unread.
